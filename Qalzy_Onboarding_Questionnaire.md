## 03: Your Customers & Competitors

### Who buys your product, and why?

*Include age, gender, location, income level, life situation, and the primary reasons they buy from you.*

Primary: women 35–65+ in the US. Overweight and actively trying to lose weight, often married, with children, some peri-menopausal, sedentary or just starting to exercise. Time-poor because of family demands. Most have a long history of losing weight and regaining it, or of not being able to shift it "no matter what". Many have low confidence and see weight loss as the route back to it, they often will not discuss their struggles openly with their own social network, so they engage anonymously and ask for advice in groups. A sizeable number have medical conditions, diabetes above all; some have had or are considering bariatric surgery; some are on or considering weight-loss drugs; some are dealing with binge eating.  Three core personas: 1\. Sophie — post-partum (25–40) or peri-menopausal (40–55) weight-loss seeker. Middle income. Hormonal and metabolic shifts mean her old strategies stopped working. Health-literate, motivated, frustrated. Cooks most meals at home. Tracks on and off rather than daily, so the tool has to be forgiving to restart. 2\. 55+ years old - Has health concerns including diabetes, cholesterol, excess weight, healthy eating, carb management, protein intake, vitamin or other nutrient intake etc. Has been told by doctor to pay attention to their nutrition. 3\. Marcus — muscle-building gym-goer, male, 35+. Protein is a priority, losing weight and building muscle. Maybe meal preps, uses a food scale and/or AI app, logs every day. Data-driven and will test accuracy before committing. 4\. Jordan — diabetic carb-counter, Type 1 or insulin-dependent Type 2\. All ages and genders. This is medical necessity, not lifestyle: an error of 10–15g of carbs has real consequences. Already owns a scale, already counts carbs, needs no onboarding. Highly motivated to pay for something reliable. 5\. Athletes, 35+ - they might run, do body building, cycling or other sports. Protein is a key priority. 6\. Longevity, metabolic health professionals/doctors and dietitians who are looking to help their patients with diet adherence (main painpoint).  Other personas include: Women during pregnancy with gestational diabetes, people that follow a specific diet such as Keto and need to count carbs.  What our Kickstarter reservee research showed (n via prelaunch.com): – Michael Carter, time-pressed fitness enthusiast — 50% of reservees. ~60% male. Age 35–44 (32%) and 25–34 (30%). Age 25–34 (40%). Income under $50k (50%).  Geography: US

### What are your customers' biggest pain points?

*What problems does your product solve for them?*

The core problem is not motivation. It is friction. Our customers have already decided to track — they have failed to keep it up. They might already be “eating healthy” but they’ve reached a weight plateau. They used to lose weight easily in the past but not any more, because of hormonal and metabolism changes around pregnancy, menopause or age (older people have muscle loss and their metabolism slows - although they don’t necessarily understand the correlation). Traditional tracking requires: weigh the food, open the app, search for the ingredient, select the right entry, enter the weight, repeat for every ingredient. That is 30+ seconds per ingredient, several times a meal, every day. People stop, weight loss stalls, and they lose motivation.  In their own words, from our research: – "I don't have time to log every meal or weigh each ingredient manually." – "Food tracking feels like a second job — too much typing, searching, and switching between apps." – "I always forget to log snacks or oils when I'm cooking." – "I'm not sure I'm logging the right portion sizes — it's all a guessing game." – "Apps don't recognise the real meals I cook." – "Tracking becomes a chore, and I eventually give up." – "My meals are complex — how can I log them without breaking them apart?"  Accuracy is the second problem. Photo-only AI calorie apps estimate portion size visually rather than measuring weight, and published research (PMID 41081011\) puts them at up to 65% inaccurate. That is enough to break a calorie deficit entirely — and it matters most for women with lower calorie requirements, people with diabetes, and athletes tracking macros.  Existing smart scales do not solve it either. They sync weight but still require manual database searching and phone use. They automate the easy part.  What Qalzy does: identifies, weighs and logs in one tap, in about four seconds, against 2m+ verified foods from USDA and CoFID — over WiFi, so the phone stays on the counter.

### What emotional benefits do customers gain from your product?

*E.g. pride, confidence, belonging, value, independence.*

– Relief from the hassle of manually calorie tracking – Relief. The thing they kept failing at is no longer effortful. – Freedom to eat what they like (including guilty pleasures) – Empowered to do the right food choices – Confidence that they know how many calories/macros/vitamins they eat, their nutrition – Confidence and better relationship with their body – Self-trust. They have abandoned tracking before; consistency rebuilds the belief that they can follow through. – Competence. It makes them feel like someone who has their health handled, rather than someone on a diet. – Freedom from restrictive dieting — visibility and control over what they already eat, rather than a meal plan imposed on them. – A little delight. It is a genuinely novel device and people enjoy showing it to others. Our brand archetypes are The Sage and The Caregiver, and the emotional benefits split along those lines.  The Sage — understanding and certainty: – "Now I understand what's happening with my body." – "I finally know what to eat, how much, and why." – "This feels smart and grounded, not gimmicky." Cutting through conflicting health advice with accurate data is itself the emotional payoff for a customer who has been given contradictory information for years.  The Caregiver — safety and support: – "This isn't about restriction, it's about care." – "It helps me take better care of myself, without stress." – "I feel supported, not shamed."  The two feelings we most want associated with the brand are Empowered Confidence and Effortless Control.

### What emotions typically drive purchasing decisions?

Frustration is the trigger — with their current app, with the effort, with a plateau, or with the feeling that their body has changed and old approaches no longer work. Hope is what converts: the belief that this time it will stick because the friction is gone.  Secondary drivers: health anxiety, particularly for the diabetic segment where accuracy is a safety issue; and a "finally, someone built this" novelty response to watching the product work in a demo.  The objections that block the sale, with weightings from our prelaunch subscriber research: – Emily Carter segment (55% of subscribers): price too high 45%, not enough information or trust 35%, unclear value compared to apps 20%. – Michael Lee segment (30%): trust 40%, price 35%, unclear value compared to apps 25%. – Sarah Mitchell segment (15%): accuracy and professional reliability 40%, cost 35%.  The specific misconceptions we hear, with our two biggest flagged: – "Why do I need a scale — can't I just use my calorie counting app?" (top concern) – "How do I know it's not a scam? How do I know this works? I’m sceptical" – "It probably won't work with complex meals and multiple ingredients on a plate. How can it tell the weight of each ingredient on a plate" – “It won’t work with my own recipes” – "AI might misidentify foods and mess up my calorie count." – "I already use MyFitnessPal — why switch?" – "Is the app free? Is there a subscription" (subscription is only for premium features, app and scale work without subscription_ – “Does it track macros (Carbs, fat, protein)” – “How does it know how many calories are in a sauce or how much oil has been used” – “How does it know to remove the weight of the bones, pith in an avocado etc” – “Can more than one people use - e.g. can my wife use it” – “How does it know what’s hidden in a sandwich”  Note that trust and price are consistently the top two across every segment.

Keen to show it to their friends.

We combine AI recognition with actual measured weight and a verified 2m+ food database.  2\.
