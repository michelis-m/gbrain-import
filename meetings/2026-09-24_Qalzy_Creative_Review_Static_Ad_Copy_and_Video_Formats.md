## Summary
Afternoon creative and strategic review session between Qalzy co-founders [[people/kostas-koukoravas]] and [[people/michael-michelis]]. The discussion centered on overcoming tactical fragmentation in content production by synthesizing 40,000 existing data points into structured buyer personas. The founders critically evaluated recent video and static ad deliverables from external agency partners, identifying major flaws in overly glossy production, UI rendering bugs, and unreadable, text-heavy static ads. They aligned on actionable design standards, including "movie trailer" style punchy static copy, in-house Figma file editing, and filming new UGC concepts including a top-down kitchen setup and a "What I Eat in a Day" rapid calorie montage.

---

## Key Discussion Points

### 1. Creative Strategy vs. Tactical Chaos
- **Strategic Gap:** Kostas highlighted the risk of operating like "headless chickens"—producing disparate ad variations without grounding them in unified persona frameworks, despite possessing over 40,000 customer data points.
- **Systematic Content Pipeline:** Emphasized mapping each creative concept to specific customer personas, identified pain points, and tested hooks (testing 10 hooks per core pain point systematically) rather than shooting in the dark.
- **Influencer Creative Freedom:** Michael balanced this by noting that for external micro-influencer partnerships, creators should retain creative freedom to execute authentic angles, with the understanding that roughly 1 in 10 creatives typically becomes a breakout performer.

### 2. Video Deliverables Critique: UI Bugs & Production Style
- **Glossy vs. Authentic UGC:** Found that recent agency video deliverables leaned too far toward an artificial, commercial "glossy look" rather than relatable UGC.
- **Screen & UI Timing Inaccuracies:**
  - In one video edit, the nutrition/calorie calculation appeared on the scale screen *before* the user even pressed the button to weigh/scan the food.
  - Scale screen text overlays were too small, rushed, and unreadable on mobile screens.
  - The "AI Camera Recognition" banner blended into the footage background; needs distinct color contrast.
- **Information Hierarchy:** Agreed that high-velocity top-of-funnel video hooks must focus solely on calories and weight rather than overcrowding the screen with complex macro distributions.

### 3. Static Ad Review & "Movie Trailer" Copy Overhaul
- **Text-Heavy Failures:** Strongly rejected recent static ad designs that featured dense paragraphs of customer review text on unappealing green and white backgrounds.
- **The "Movie Trailer" Standard:** Established that static ads must mimic theatrical review trailers—using high-impact 2- to 3-word statements that can be absorbed in under 2 seconds:
  - Examples: *"Life-changing"*, *"Effortless"*, *"Finally Accurate"*, *"No More Guessing"*.
  - Strict guideline: Maximum 2 short lines of text per static creative.
- **Direct Figma Revisions:** Agreed to take the raw Figma project files and modify typography, copy, and layouts internally rather than waiting on flawed agency iterations.

### 4. New UGC Video Formats to Shoot
- **Top-Down Kitchen Demo:** Michael proposed setting up a top-down overhead camera rig in his Marousi kitchen to film authentic ingredient weighing and AI recognition from the cook's perspective.
- **"What I Eat in a Day" Rapid Montage:** Concept featuring rapid-fire cuts of diverse meals placed on the scale throughout a day, prominently displaying ascending calorie totals (e.g. 500 kcal breakfast, 1,200 kcal lunch, 2,000 kcal total daily intake) to demonstrate effortless daily tracking.
- **Gram-by-Gram Precision:** Highlight scale sensitivity by showing micro-adjustments (e.g. adding single ingredients and watching the readout tick up cleanly by 6g increments).
