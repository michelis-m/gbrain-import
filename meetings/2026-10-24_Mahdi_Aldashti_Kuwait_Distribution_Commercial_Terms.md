# Qalzy <> Mahdi Aldashti: Kuwait Commercial Distribution Agreement, Pricing & Localization

**Date:** 2026-10-24  
**Attendees:** [[people/michael-michelis]], [[people/kostas-koukoravas]], [[people/mahdi-aldashti]]  
**Companies:** [[companies/qalzy]]  

---

## Executive Summary
Michael Michelis and Kostas Koukoravas met with Mahdi Aldashti to finalize commercial distribution terms for Qalzy in Kuwait. Following consultations with his company owner, Mahdi pushed for a reduced introductory wholesale price of $75/unit for an initial 250-unit pilot, citing local retail margin pressure and high marketing costs. The founders agreed to an introductory rate of $75/unit for the first 250 units, while establishing that future orders will enforce higher MOQ tiers (500+ units) to maintain that pricing. Key operational alignments were reached on Arabic app localization, RF/CE certifications for Kuwait Citra clearance, and a local replacement warranty model. A contentious negotiation took place over subscription revenue sharing, resulting in an agreement to present two pricing options (with and without shared IT/cloud operational costs).

---

## Key Discussion Points

### 1. Business Scale, Production & Financial Stability
- **Current Traction & Runway:** Mahdi inquired about Qalzy's recent commercial run-rate and upcoming fundraising plans. Kostas explained that Qalzy generated ~$250,000 during its initial Kickstarter campaign and is currently doing ~$50,000/month (~250 units/month) since direct sales launched in July/August. Kostas clarified that Qalzy operates profitably and self-sufficiently, meaning any future funding round would be opportunistic to accelerate global expansion rather than out of financial necessity.
- **Production Lead Times:** Lead times for manufacturing batches run 2.5 to 3 months. While Qalzy currently holds sufficient buffer inventory to fulfill Mahdi's pilot batch immediately from stock, larger volume replenishment orders will require advance scheduling.

### 2. Software Localization & Computer Vision Capabilities
- **Arabic Translation:** Mahdi emphasized that an Arabic interface is critical for mainstream adoption across Kuwait and the Gulf. Michael confirmed that Qalzy already supports 10 languages and has the infrastructure to deploy an AI-translated Arabic build quickly, with Mahdi offering to review and QA the translation.
- **Middle Eastern Food Recognition:** Mahdi raised queries regarding local Kuwaiti dishes (shawarma, machboos). Kostas clarified that Qalzy utilizes Google Gemini's multimodal cloud model, which has extensive training across global cuisines.
- **Cooking Method Heuristics:** Mahdi requested a UI toggle or preset button to hint cooking preparation (e.g., baked vs. fried, lean vs. standard fats) to avoid calorie miscalculations. Michael and Kostas noted that while immediate overrides can be handled by tapping and editing the item, upcoming model upgrades and features (like custom user favorite presets and cooking method prompts) will be prioritized against broader user feedback from Qalzy's ~2,000 active users.

### 3. Regulatory Compliance & Local Customer Warranty
- **Kuwait Citra & Wireless Certifications:** Mahdi noted that Kuwait's telecommunications authority (Citra) requires proof of wireless compliance. Kostas and Michael confirmed Qalzy has full CE, UKCA, FCC, FDA, and RF/electromagnetic compatibility certifications across Europe, the US, Canada, and Australia, and agreed to supply all documentation.
- **Defective Unit RMA Workflow:** For broken or malfunctioning units, customers will email `customercontact@qalzy.com` with video verification. If troubleshooting fails, Mahdi will replace the unit directly from local stock in Kuwait. Qalzy will maintain an RMA log and credit/reimburse the cost of replaced units on Mahdi's subsequent wholesale purchase order.

### 4. Subscription Revenue Sharing Negotiation
- **Revenue Share Request:** Mahdi proposed receiving a 30% revenue share on all recurring app subscriptions activated by Kuwait buyers.
- **Founders' Firm Rebuttal:** Kostas rejected unburdened subscription sharing, pointing out that Apple takes 30% off the top and Qalzy absorbs 100% of the ongoing lifetime expenses: cloud infrastructure, Gemini AI multimodal token costs for unlimited lifetime food scans, ongoing software engineering, email marketing, and customer support. Offering free lifetime scanning while sharing pure subscription upside without sharing overhead was deemed unacceptable.
- **Compromise Framework:** The parties agreed to formulate two distinct contractual proposals:
  - *Option A:* Hardware-only wholesale purchase ($75/unit intro) with zero subscription revenue sharing.
  - *Option B:* Hardware purchase coupled with a subscription revenue share, but where the wholesale unit price or ongoing fees incorporate an allocated IT, server, and AI token operational cost.

### 5. Unit Economics, Retail Strategy & MOQ Pricing Structure
- **Wholesale Price Concession:** Mahdi presented a detailed cost breakdown based on advice from his company owner (whose firm generates ~$2M/month in Kuwait). Arguing that Kuwait's calorie-counting demographic is small and that entry-level fitness consumers (ages 16–17) require accessible pricing, Mahdi requested a $75/unit price (noting his owner initially pushed for $60). At a $160 retail price, factoring in ~$100 landed costs (unit + air freight + 5% airport customs) and a 30% cut for local retail/gym partners, Mahdi claimed he nets only ~$20/unit profit ($5,000 on 250 units) while planning $20,000 in creator marketing.
- **Introductory Compromise:** Michael and Kostas agreed to grant **$75.00 USD / unit** for an initial pilot MOQ of **250 units** as a one-time introductory offer.
- **Per-Order MOQ Enforcement:** Michael and Kostas firmly established that future orders will not honor annual cumulative volumes; discounts are strictly tied to per-order volume tiers (e.g., subsequent orders at $75 will require a 500+ unit MOQ; 1,000+ unit tiers will receive deeper volume discounts).
- **Retail Floor Advice:** Kostas and Michael strongly advised Mahdi against capping retail at $160–$169, recommending he launch at $199 to preserve healthy margin buffers for distributor discounts, gym margins, and targeted promotional segmentation.
