## Summary
Michael Michelis and Kostas Koukoravas met with Mila Huang, an analyst at the Harvard Undergraduate Venture Capital Group (VCG), for an exploratory investment sourcing discussion covering Qalzy's origin, hardware-enabled AI software model, unit economics, supply chain, and growth strategy.

---

## Key Discussion Points

### 1. Company Background & Mission
- Michael and Kostas shared Qalzy's founding story: addressing the friction and inaccuracy of manual calorie logging by building a smart kitchen scale with an integrated optical camera and computer vision models that automatically weigh, recognize, and log food.
- Outlined user demographics: fitness enthusiasts, macro counters, people managing metabolic conditions, and health-conscious home cooks seeking frictionless tracking.

### 2. Business Model & Unit Economics
- Explained the hybrid business model combining direct-to-consumer hardware sales (99–39 price point) with recurring high-margin app software subscriptions (18% organic attach rate).
- Discussed manufacturing logistics via China, fulfillment through Portless, and planned retail/Amazon expansion.

### 3. VCG Sourcing Process & Timeline
- Mila explained the Harvard VCG process:
  - She will synthesize her notes and pitch Qalzy to her internal VCG investment committee.
  - If approved, Qalzy will be featured in the quarterly VCG Deal Book distributed to partner tier-1 venture capital firms (including Insight Partners, a16z, F-Prime, Eniac) ahead of the winter syndicate.
  - VCG leadership and partner funds will reach out directly for formal partner-level meetings if selected.
