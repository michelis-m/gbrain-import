# Meta Ads Scaling, Auditing, and Algorithmic Principles (SPG Framework)

A comprehensive strategic and diagnostic framework on Meta's ad auction, machine learning algorithms, creative velocity, and full-ecosystem DTC media buying, synthesized from Social Paradigm Group's teardowns of 200+ ad accounts, high-spend brand audits, and Meta leadership roundtables.

Relevant entities: [[companies/qalzy]], [[people/michael-michelis]].

---

## 1. Algorithmic Realities & The "Never Turn Off High-Volume Ads" Rule

### Objective Alignment: Highest Volume vs. Cost Caps
- **The Core Conflict:** Over 95% of Meta advertisers optimize for **Highest Volume** (maximizing conversion volume across the campaign). However, media buyers often manually pause ads based on arbitrary in-platform CPA/ROAS targets that exist only in their heads.
- **Estimated Action Rate (EAR):** Meta optimizes across billions of data points using Estimated Action Rate ($pCTR \times pCVR$). The ad receiving the most spend is the ad Meta has identified as capable of maintaining volume and conversion density.
- **Spend as the Leading Indicator:** Under Highest Volume, the top-spending ad is your true "winner." Sorting and evaluating by in-platform ROAS prioritizes low-spend, low-volume harvesters over high-volume customer acquisition engines.

### The "Hamster Wheel of Decline"
When media buyers manually kill high-spend volume drivers because of in-platform CPA:
1. **Budget Reallocation:** Meta shifts spend down into lower-funnel retargeting ads, catalog/DPA ads, or static promotional offers.
2. **First-Time Impression Ratio (FTIR) Collapses:** Top-of-funnel reach drops sharply, and ad frequency spikes.
3. **Account-Wide Decay:** Because net-new customer acquisition is starved, blended business revenue immediately plummets.
4. **Panic Cycle:** The buyer restarts the paused ads or cuts even more ads, locking the account into constant performance degradation.

### In-Ad ROAS vs. Incremental Business Growth
- As confirmed by Meta data science leads: **The ads inside an ad account with the highest ROAS are usually the least incremental.**
- High-ROAS ads (e.g., catalog ads, coupon statics) sit near the bottom of the funnel capturing conversions that top-of-funnel ads (e.g., Reels, brand hooks) generated.
- Top-of-funnel ads often show lower in-platform ROAS (e.g., 0.8x) while driving high blended store revenue (e.g., 3.0x blended ROAS).
- Meta's 7-day click / 1-day view attribution window cannot track delayed purchases outside the window or cross-SKU attribution accurately.

### The Only Two Legitimate Reasons to Pause an Ad
1. The featured SKU or product is out of stock.
2. A temporary promo or seasonal offer has ended (which should ideally be housed in a separate promotional campaign).

## 2. The ROAS & CPA Paradox

### You Don't Actually Want a Higher ROAS
- As ad spend scales, in-platform ROAS **naturally compresses**. Forcing a fixed or increasing ROAS while increasing budget forces the algorithm into hyper-narrow retargeting.
- **Contribution Margin ($) over Percentages:** The goal of an e-commerce business is maximizing total cash profit ($ Contribution Margin), not percentage ROAS. A 2.0x ROAS on £100k spend generates far more cash than a 4.0x ROAS on £10k spend.

### Why You Actually Need a Higher CPA
- Brands obsessed with driving down CPA end up bidding only for bottom-funnel, discount-seeking buyers.
- Accepting a higher allowable CPA allows you to outspend competitors on top-of-funnel acquisition, acquire higher-AOV buyers, and capture greater market share.
- If a brand cannot tolerate CPA variance, switch to **Cost Caps (Cost per result goal)** so Meta enforces the ceiling natively.

## 3. Attribution, Incrementality & Ecosystem Thinking

### Attribution Distortions & The Incrementality Problem
- All single-platform attribution models distort reality. Multi-touch user journeys span Meta, Google, TikTok, email, and organic search.
- Meta creates demand at the top of the funnel; other channels (Google Search, Amazon, TikTok Shop, Direct) capture that demand.
- Evaluating Meta in isolation leads brands to mistakenly cut top-of-funnel spend, collapsing downstream channel volume.
- **Holistic Evaluation:** Judge channel efficacy using **Blended MER (Total Net Store Revenue ÷ Total Marketing Spend)** and **Contribution Margin (CM)**.

## 4. Creative Production, Velocity & Testing Strategy

### Creative Velocity Trumps Hit Rates
- Creative hit rates in high-performing accounts sit around **5% to 10%**. Looking for high hit rates is counterproductive.
- Success is driven by **testing velocity**: brands testing 50–100+ concepts per month discover the 1–2% outlier volume drivers that hold 70–80% of spend and power the business.

### Creative Diversity Beats Minor Visual Iteration
- Testing minor tweaks (font changes, subtle color swaps) yields negligible variance.
- High-impact creative testing requires fundamentally diverse:
  1. **Awareness Angles:** Unaware, Problem-Aware, Solution-Aware.
  2. **Hooks:** Visual pattern interrupts, controversial claims, direct callouts.
  3. **Creative Formats:** Short-form video/Reels, UGC, PR/founder stories, side-by-side comparison statics, dynamic product ads (DPA).
- **Customer Focus:** "Nobody cares about your brand" — ads must focus on solving the customer's problem or fulfilling their emotional desire.

### Partnership Ads (Creator Whitelisting)
- Running ads from creator/influencer handles removes corporate ad skepticism, lowers CPCs, expands First-Time Impression Ratio (FTIR), and increases conversion rates.

## 5. Account Architecture & Learning Phase Truths

### Account Consolidation Blueprint
- **1 Consolidated BAU Campaign:** Open broad targeting (Age, Gender, Location only). Let the creative do the targeting.
- **1 Creative Testing Sandbox:** Dynamic Creative Testing (DCT) or structured test ad sets (3–6 creatives each) funded with at least $50+/day.
- Eliminate audience fragmentation (avoid stacking dozens of lookalikes or narrow interest groups).

### The Truth About the Learning Phase
- **Continuous Adaptation:** Audiences and creatives fatigue naturally; the algorithm is in constant learning.
- **50 Conversions/Week:** An optimization benchmark, not a prerequisite for stability.
- **Diagnosing "Learning Limited":** Overwhelmingly caused by **budget fragmentation** across too many campaigns/ad sets. Consolidate architecture to fix it.
- **Safe Budget Changes:** Shifts within $\pm 20\%$ generally avoid triggering a hard learning reset.

## 6. Financial & Operational Scaling Realities

- **Marketing and Finance Alignment:** Align performance marketing goals with CFO metrics: Contribution Margin after COGS, fulfillment, payment fees, and returns.
- **The Black Friday Trap:** Heavy discount-driven Black Friday surges often result in January cash crunches, bloated return rates, and diminished brand equity. Focus on sustainable year-round full-price customer acquisition.
- **Cash Conversion Cycle:** Scale spend only at a rate your cash conversion cycle and working capital can support.

---

## 7. Systematic Account Audit Diagnostic Checklist

1. **Architecture & Consolidation:** Verify if budget is spread across >3–5 campaigns or stuck in *Learning Limited*. Consolidate into 1 BAU + 1 Creative Sandbox.
2. **Bidding Alignment:** Verify Highest Volume vs Cost Caps. Stop manual ad cuts on Highest Volume.
3. **Spend Distribution:** Sort ads by spend (descending). Identify volume anchors. Check if top spenders were recently paused.
4. **Soft Metrics Health:** Check FTIR (>70–80%), account frequency, and CTR/CPC.
5. **Creative Velocity:** Audit monthly concept throughput and format diversity (video/Reels vs statics vs Partnership ads).
6. **Ecosystem & Blended Health:** Compare in-ad ROAS against blended MER, contribution margin dollars, and return rates.
